Editor’s Handbook

How to add and look after everything on Liberty Lighthouse: FAQs, videos, topics and their syllabi, glossary terms, reader comments, images and site settings. Every step here was carried out for real in the CMS, and every screenshot shows what happened.

Prepared by Adnan, Thothica, for the Centre for Civil Society · November 2026
Chapter 1

Before you start

Liberty Lighthouse has a content management system (the CMS) at /admin on the site. You use it to write and change everything readers see. You never need to touch code or files.

This handbook lives in the CMS

Click Handbook in the CMS’s top bar, or the Editor’s Handbook link under the sign-in button, and it opens in a new tab. Its address is /admin/handbook/ on the site.

What happens when you click Publish

The CMS keeps every entry in CCS’s GitHub account, the same place the website’s code lives. Publishing saves your entry there, and the site then rebuilds itself with your change.

YouClick PublishThe CMS saves the entry.
SecondsSaved to GitHubRecorded under your account, with the date and what changed.
About 2 minutesSite rebuildsVercel, the host, rebuilds every page.
DoneLiveReaders see the change. Refresh the page to see it.
Important

There is no review step. Publish means live, about two minutes later. The CMS’s own notes mention a “Draft → In Review → Publish” workflow, but that review stage is not switched on. To work on something privately, switch on its Draft switch before you publish. A draft is saved but readers cannot see it.

The rules that matter most

  • Draft firstSwitch Draft on for anything unfinished. Readers never see drafts.
  • Check the site after publishingWait two minutes, open the page on the site and read it once.
  • Describe every imageFill in the image’s Alt text. Without it the image is invisible to blind readers and search engines.
  • Never Heading 1The question or title is already the page heading. Use Heading 2 for sections and Heading 3 below that.
  • Get the question right firstAn FAQ’s web address comes from its question the first time it is published, and never changes after that.
  • Practise freelyMake a practice entry with “Practice” in its title, keep it as a draft, and delete it afterwards (chapter 14).

Chapter 2

Signing in

What you need

You sign in with a GitHub account that has write access to CCS’s Liberty Lighthouse repository (CentreforCivilSociety-Academy/liberty-lighthouse). CCS’s editors currently use the shared CentreforCivilSociety-Academy account. A new team member either uses that account or has their own GitHub account added to the repository with the Write role. Whoever manages CCS’s GitHub account does this, under the repository’s Settings → Collaborators and teams.

Steps

  1. Open www.libertylighthouse.in/admin/. If that address does not load, use liberty-lighthouse.vercel.app/admin/, which is the same CMS.
  2. Click Login with GitHub. A small window opens. If nothing happens, your browser blocked the pop-up: allow pop-ups for the site and click again.
  3. Sign in to GitHub in that window.
  4. The first time only, GitHub asks you to authorise Liberty Lighthouse CMS to use your account. Click Authorize. The window closes and the CMS opens.
Signing out

Click the round account icon at the top right, then Log Out. Sign out on shared computers.

Chapter 3

Finding your way around

After signing in you land on the dashboard. The numbers in the orange circles on each screenshot match the numbers in its caption.

Click a collection to see its entries. Each collection has the same controls.

The editor

Clicking an entry opens the editor. The left side holds the fields, each with a short hint underneath. The right side is a preview of how the entry will look, updated as you type.

The status under the collection name tells you where you stand:

  • CHANGES SAVED (green): everything on screen is saved.
  • UNSAVED CHANGES (red): you changed something that is not saved yet. Publish to save it. If you leave the page, the browser asks “Are you sure you want to leave this page?”. Answer Cancel to stay and keep your work.
The preview is a guide

The preview shows the content and its formatting, but not exactly the finished page. Always check the real page on the site after publishing. Topic previews, for example, show the syllabus as plain text with its ## marks.

Chapter 4

Adding an FAQ

This chapter follows one practice FAQ from a blank form to a live page. It uses every formatting tool once, so you can see what each one does.

Start a new FAQ

Topic, question and order

  • Question. Write it in full, with its question mark, exactly as readers should see it. It also sets the page’s web address, so get it right before the first publish. Why do government schools underperform? becomes /topics/education/faq/why-do-government-schools-underperform/.
  • Display order. Lower numbers come first in the topic’s list. Number in tens (10, 20, 30) so you can slot a new FAQ in later without renumbering the others.

Draft

Switch Draft on while you write. You can publish as often as you like and nothing appears on the site until you switch it off.

Related FAQs and videos

These add a Related Resources box at the foot of the page. They are optional. You can link to any FAQ or video, in any topic.

Writing the answer

Write as you would in a word processor: Enter starts a new paragraph. If you paste from Word or Google Docs, check the formatting in the preview afterwards, especially headings.

Links

  1. Select the words that should become the link.
  2. Click the Link button (the chain icon).
  3. A small box from your browser asks “Enter the URL of the link”. Paste the full address, starting with https://, and click OK.

Links to other websites open in a new tab on the site automatically.

Images

Prepare the image first: 800 to 1200 pixels wide and under 200 KB. A JPG suits photographs and a PNG suits charts.

Write real alt text

Alt text is what a blind reader hears in place of the picture, and what search engines read. Describe what the image shows and its main point. “A bar chart showing that 70 per cent of families pay less than 1,000 rupees a month in school fees” is useful. “Chart” or the file name is not.

Put the image’s source on its own line straight after it, in italics, starting with Source:. Link the source’s name to where it came from, as the existing FAQs do.

Tables

Never upload a picture of a table. A picture cannot be read aloud, searched or resized on a phone. Turn it into a real table instead:

  1. Paste the table image into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini with this prompt:
Convert this table image into a plain HTML table. Only output the <table> element with <thead>, <tbody>, <th>, and <td> tags. Do not include any CSS, classes, or styling attributes.
  1. Check the numbers against the original. AI tools sometimes misread a digit.
  2. In the answer, flip the Rich Text / Markdown switch to Markdown, click where the table should go, and paste.
  3. Flip back to Rich Text. The table’s code stays visible as plain lines in the editor, which is normal. The preview and the site show a proper table. Leave those lines alone in Rich Text mode, and edit them only in Markdown mode.

Publish

Because Draft was on, the page does not exist on the site yet:

When the FAQ is ready, switch Draft off and publish again:

Share images are automatic

Each FAQ gets its own share card, the picture shown when someone posts the link on WhatsApp, LinkedIn or X. Leave the Share image field at the top of the form empty unless you want to replace that card (chapter 11).

Chapter 5

Editing, hiding and deleting

Edit

The address keeping its original form is deliberate: links people have already shared keep working. If an address really must change, tell Thothica, who can add a redirect from the old address.

Changing an FAQ’s Topic moves it to the new topic’s list and changes its address to the new topic.

Duplicate

When the CMS refuses to save

The notice reads “Oops, you’ve missed a required field. Please complete before saving.” Scroll up to find the field marked in red. Fields refuse a value in the wrong format in the same way, for example a full YouTube link where only the code belongs. The message under the field says what it wants.

Hide or delete

  • To take a page down for now, switch on Draft and publish. The page disappears from the site, and you can bring it back by switching Draft off.
  • To remove it for good, use Delete entry. The browser asks “Are you sure you want to delete this published entry?”. Click OK.
Deleting is a last resort

Every link to the deleted page, from other sites and from shared messages, then lands on “Page not found”. Images uploaded into the FAQ are not deleted with it. If a deletion was a mistake, Thothica can restore the entry from GitHub’s history.

Chapter 7

Topics and guided syllabi

A topic is a theme, such as Education or Agriculture. Every FAQ and video belongs to one. You will mostly come here to edit a topic’s guided syllabus, its reading list. Add a new topic only when CCS starts a new theme.

A new topic

Choose the URL slug once

The URL slug is in the address of the topic and of every FAQ and video under it. Use the topic name in lowercase, with hyphens for spaces: property-rights, not Property Rights. Never change it after FAQs have been added to the topic, or all their addresses change.

Writing the guided syllabus

Follow the pattern the existing syllabi use: a Heading 2 for each kind of resource, then a numbered list. Each item is the title as a link, then a dash and the author.

## Books

1. [The Beautiful Tree](https://www.amazon.in/dp/B00ELPRLC0) — James Tooley

## Academic Papers

1. [Teacher Absence in India: A Snapshot](https://academic.oup.com/…) — Michael Kremer and others

Use the section names the other topics use, so the syllabi read alike: Books, Academic Papers, Reports, Articles & Blog Posts and Podcasts & Videos, plus Legal Cases or Legal Statutes where a topic needs them. Do not make a heading bold as well: it is already a heading.

On the site

Before deleting a topic

Move its FAQs and videos to another topic, or delete them, first. When the practice deletion was tested, the topic’s FAQ pages stayed online but vanished from every list, so readers could reach them only by direct link or search.

Chapter 8

Glossary

A glossary term does two jobs. It gets its own page under /glossary/. And wherever the term or one of its aliases appears in an FAQ or a video’s notes, the site turns it into a link with a pop-up definition, automatically.

An alias can belong to only one term

If two terms share a word, for example two terms both listing “Minimum Support Price”, the site cannot be rebuilt. It stays on its last good version, and nobody’s changes go live until the clash is removed. Before adding an alias, search the Glossary for it. This was tested: the clash stopped the build with “alias collision”.

On the site

How the automatic links behave

  • Only the first mention on each page becomes a link, so pages do not fill up with dotted underlines.
  • Capitals do not matter: MSP, msp and Msp all match.
  • Mentions inside headings, existing links and code are left alone.
  • A term never links to itself on its own page.
  • Draft terms do not link anywhere.
  • To stop one mention linking, so the link falls on a better mention further down, switch the answer to Markdown mode and wrap the words like this: <span data-no-gloss>MSP</span>.

Chapter 9

Moderating comments

Readers can comment under any FAQ, video or glossary term after signing in with GitHub. Nothing appears until someone at CCS approves it.

How a comment reaches you

  1. A reader writes a comment on the site and submits it.
  2. Within a minute or two, an automatic process files it in the CMS under Comments, marked PENDING.
  3. You approve, reject or delete it. Check for pending comments regularly: readers are told comments are reviewed.

The three choices

ChoiceHowUse it for
ApproveSet Status to approved, then Publish. Live in about two minutes.Comments that are on topic and civil, including ones that disagree with CCS.
RejectSet Status to rejected, then Publish. Stays hidden, and a record is kept.Off-topic or low-quality comments that are not abusive.
DeleteDelete entry, then OK. Removed from the CMS entirely.Spam, abuse, harassment, personal details, anything that should not be kept.
  • Comments are plain text. Formatting marks show as typed.
  • You may fix an obvious typo in the Body field. Never rewrite what someone said. Reject or delete instead.
  • Leave the page, name, GitHub username and dates as they are.
  • For severe content, also ask the GitHub account’s manager to delete the original submission on GitHub. Its number is in the comment’s Issue number field. Deleting in the CMS does not remove that copy.

Chapter 10

Images and the Media library

The Media tab at the top holds the site’s shared images, the folder the earlier FAQs’ images live in.

Where images end up

  • An image uploaded with Choose an image inside an FAQ answer is stored alongside that FAQ. It does not appear in the Media tab.
  • To reuse an image from the Media tab in an answer, select it in Media, click Copy Path, then in the image block click Insert from URL and paste.
  • Share images (chapter 11) go in a folder of their own.
  • Deleting an entry does not delete its images. Delete an image from Media only when you are sure no page uses it: a page using a deleted image shows a broken picture.

Chapter 11

Site Settings and share images

Site Settings change the whole site at once, at the next rebuild. Most never need touching. Change one thing at a time, then check the site.

The fonts are fixed to the site’s faces (EB Garamond for headings, Roboto for text). The sizes can move within set limits. Each hint says which parts of the site a setting affects, and gives its default value so you can put it back.

There are two kinds of bot. Training bots learn from the site to improve AI models. Search and chat bots fetch pages to answer someone’s question, with a link back to the site. Allowing all of them helps CCS’s work reach people who ask AI tools about Indian policy. Blocking Google-Extended does not affect Google Search.

Share images

Every page gets a share card made automatically. You can replace one with your own design:

  1. Open /tools/og on the site (for example liberty-lighthouse.vercel.app/tools/og).
  2. Type the eyebrow, headline and button text, and pick a colour.
  3. Click Download PNG. The card is 1200 × 630 pixels, the size every platform expects.
  4. Upload it to the Share image field at the top of the FAQ, video or topic. For the home, about, glossary, search and AI pages, use Site Settings → Share images instead.

WhatsApp and LinkedIn remember a link’s card for some days, so a new card may not show at once in places where the link was shared before.

Chapter 13

When something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
My change is not on the siteWait two to three minutes, then refresh. On a phone, close and reopen the tab. Check that Draft is off. If it is still missing after ten minutes, the rebuild may have failed (next row).
Nobody’s changes are going liveA rebuild failed. The site stays on its last good version. The usual cause is two glossary terms sharing an alias (chapter 8). Think about what was published just before the problem started, and tell Thothica.
“Oops, you’ve missed a required field”A required field is empty. Scroll up to the field marked in red.
A field will not accept what I typedRead the red message under it. Usual causes: a full YouTube link instead of the code, a duration not in 18:42 form, a slug with capitals or spaces, a definition over 280 characters, a web address without https://.
Login with GitHub does nothingAllow pop-ups for the site in your browser, then try again.
I am signed in but cannot saveYour GitHub account may not have write access to the repository. Ask whoever manages CCS’s GitHub account.
Two of us edited the same entryThe second save can fail or overwrite the first. Agree who edits what. Reload an entry before editing it if a colleague may have changed it.
The site address does not loadUse liberty-lighthouse.vercel.app, which is the same site and the same CMS, and tell Thothica.
An image does not appearCheck the image block has an image chosen, not only alt text. Check it was not deleted from Media.
I deleted something by mistakeTell Thothica straight away, with the title and roughly when. Every change is kept in GitHub’s history, so it can be restored.

For anything else, contact Thothica with the page’s address, what you did, and a screenshot.
